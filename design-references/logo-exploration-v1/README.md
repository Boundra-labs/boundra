# Boundra Logo Exploration v1

Four initial logo directions for review. These are image-generated concept references, not production vector masters. The selected direction should later be redrawn on a geometric grid, optically corrected, and exported as SVG.

No application code was changed.

## Shared requirements

- Brand name: `boundra`, lowercase.
- Tone: calm, capable, transparent, and self-custodial.
- Primary palette remains forest, ivory, sage, and a restrained lime accent.
- Marks must remain recognizable at favicon size.
- Avoid generic crypto imagery: coins, chains, shields, locks, nodes, and market charts.
- Avoid visual claims that imply guaranteed security or returns.

## Concepts

### 1. Open Boundary Monogram

File: `01-open-boundary-monogram.png`

A lowercase `b` built from a vertical stem and segmented circular boundary. The lime terminal marks the point where authority stops.

Strengths:

- Most recognizable as an initial.
- Strong favicon and app-icon potential.
- Connects directly to the segmented mascot ring.
- Easy to animate as segments opening or closing.

Risks:

- Requires careful optical correction so the segments do not resemble a loading spinner.
- The lime segment should remain small to preserve a serious fintech tone.

Best use: primary corporate mark if compact recognition is the priority.

### 2. Living Boundary Symbol

File: `02-living-boundary-symbol.png`

Three open arcs organize around a stable center. The negative space subtly suggests `b`, while the core represents user intent or owner authority.

Strengths:

- Most premium and abstract direction.
- Closely supports the Living Boundary homepage concept.
- Communicates bounded motion without relying on a literal security symbol.
- Works well in light and dark environments.

Risks:

- The central dot and arcs must be tuned to avoid looking like a target or generic orbit.
- The symbol needs a consistent orientation; it should never rotate continuously.

Best use: primary brand identity if Boundra should feel sophisticated and product-led.

### 3. Boundary Spirit Emblem

File: `03-boundary-spirit-emblem.png`

A flat reduction of the approved mascot: teardrop silhouette, two eyes, and segmented boundary ring.

Strengths:

- Strongest connection between product identity and mascot.
- Immediately friendly and distinctive.
- Natural choice for social avatars, onboarding, and community material.

Risks:

- More playful than the other directions.
- At very small sizes the eyes and ring segments may need a specially simplified micro-mark.
- Should not replace the full mascot in expressive illustrations.

Best use: companion brand mark, community icon, or primary mark if approachability is the main goal.

### 4. Flowline Wordmark

File: `04-flowline-wordmark.png`

A custom lowercase wordmark crossed by a continuous line that deliberately stops at a lime terminal.

Strengths:

- Most directly aligned with the flowing homepage motion language.
- Distinctive without requiring a separate icon in large contexts.
- The stopping terminal communicates controlled autonomy.

Risks:

- The line can reduce legibility at small sizes.
- Requires the most typographic refinement and responsive lockup variants.
- A simplified `b` favicon is still necessary.

Best use: campaign or editorial wordmark, or a primary wordmark paired with a simpler symbol.

## Recommendation

The strongest primary direction is **Concept 2: Living Boundary Symbol**, paired with a clean lowercase wordmark. It fits both the product thesis and the newer flowing homepage without becoming too literal or playful.

Concept 1 is the strongest alternative when favicon recognition and technical clarity matter most. Concept 3 can remain the mascot/community emblem even if it is not chosen as the corporate logo. Concept 4's line treatment can inform motion and section dividers without being used in every small lockup.

## Suggested refinement after selection

1. Rebuild the chosen mark as true vector geometry.
2. Test at 16, 24, 32, 48, and 128 pixels.
3. Produce horizontal, stacked, symbol-only, and wordmark-only lockups.
4. Create forest-on-ivory, ivory-on-forest, and one-color variants.
5. Define clear space and minimum size.
6. Test against the homepage header, wallet button, favicon, social avatar, and loading state.
7. Check visual similarity and naming/trademark risk before public release.

## Generation briefs

The concepts were produced with the built-in image-generation tool.

- **Open Boundary Monogram:** segmented lowercase `b`, small lime stopping point, horizontal and compact lockups.
- **Living Boundary Symbol:** three open arcs around a stable core with a subtle negative-space `b`.
- **Boundary Spirit Emblem:** flat reduction of the approved mascot identity and ring.
- **Flowline Wordmark:** exact lowercase `boundra` lettering crossed by a continuous line ending at a lime terminal.
